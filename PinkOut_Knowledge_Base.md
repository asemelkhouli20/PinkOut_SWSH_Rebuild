# PinkOut — Complete Project Knowledge Base

> **What this file is:** An independent, descriptive source of knowledge about **PinkOut**: its identity, purpose, physical world, experiences, audience, programming, business context, website and app roles, creative direction, history, and unanswered questions. Its purpose is to give an AI, designer, writer, product team, partner, or developer an accurate mental model of PinkOut before they work on any PinkOut-related task.
>
> **What this file is not:** A website build brief, a list of mandatory site sections, a coding prompt, a ticket sales page, a live events database, a verified construction report, or an assertion that planned activities are already operating. Tasks and implementation requirements should be specified separately. This knowledge should remain useful beyond any one website or app project.

## 1. Identity and Executive Understanding

### 1.1 The central definition

**PinkOut is a women-first lifestyle, creativity, entertainment, and community destination being developed for Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. It brings together beauty, movement, hands-on making, shopping, live performances, food and café moments, emerging talent, and changing interactive activities inside one distinctive PinkOut world.**

The physical destination is the first major expression of PinkOut, but its vision is broader than a single event. The brand seeks to grow into a returning destination and an ongoing platform through its mobile app, creators, partnerships, programs, and future activations.

### 1.2 A concise explanation

A visitor comes to PinkOut to **do things**, not only to look around or buy products. She might arrive through an immersive art entrance, meet friends over matcha, try a beauty experience, personalize an accessory, discover gifts and fashion, join a dance class, and watch a live talk or performance. On another visit, the pop-ups, themed day, workshops, guest creators, and stage content may be different. PinkOut retains one recognizable identity while its reasons to return change.

### 1.3 What makes PinkOut a distinct concept

- **An experience world:** Visitors encounter multiple connected, differentiated environments rather than a single narrow service.
- **Participation:** Making, trying, playing, moving, recording, learning, and interacting matter as much as passive viewing.
- **Community:** The concept creates a venue and a digital relationship for women, friends, creators, instructors, and brands.
- **Continuous renewal:** Rotating themes and activations make repeat visits central to the idea.
- **One memorable visual identity:** Pink, deep green, warm ivory, arches, sculptural furniture, light, and campaign imagery tie together otherwise varied zones.
- **Physical plus digital:** The destination hosts experiences; the app supports discovery, schedules, reservations, and ongoing connection.
- **Commercial flexibility:** Sponsors, retailers, instructors, product makers, and event partners may participate through several different models.

### 1.4 What PinkOut is not

PinkOut is **not** merely a beauty salon, spa, makeup marketplace, perfume retailer, fashion shop, café chain, kids' playground, single concert, conference, or at-home beauty provider. Some such elements may appear **inside** PinkOut or in longer-term research, but none by itself defines the brand. In particular, the separate Brush Touch-inspired beauty-marketplace research does **not** make PinkOut an already operating beauty-professional booking marketplace.

## 2. Vision, Mission, and Value

### 2.1 Vision

The primary concept material describes PinkOut as **a year-round destination rather than a one-time event**. This is an ambition about the brand's continuity; it does not confirm 365-day operations, a permanent lease, or a fully finalized schedule. The emphasis is on a recognizable place that evolves and makes visitors want to come back. The app supports continuity before, during, and after a season or event.

### 2.2 Three pillars

**Experience.** Interactive and personalized moments in which visitors actively participate: beauty experimentation, making, movement, entertainment, art installations, and special activities.

**Community.** Opportunities for women, friends, artists, instructors, designers, performers, and creators to meet, learn, collaborate, showcase talent, and develop longer-term relationships.

**Commercial opportunity.** A flexible environment for businesses and individuals to activate, demonstrate, sell, host, teach, perform, sponsor, or engage directly with audiences.

These three pillars are interdependent. Experience attracts participation; community creates belonging and repeat visits; commerce supports the changing physical and creative program.

### 2.3 The emotional promise

The PinkOut experience is designed to generate **curiosity, self-expression, social belonging, joy, inspiration, and anticipation of something new**. It can feel intimate and welcoming in its café or studios, then energetic and expressive at the live stage or during a themed day. It should feel fashionable and aspirational without becoming inaccessible or infantilizing its audience.

### 2.4 User needs it addresses

People want meaningful places to go with friends, memorable content and photo moments, opportunities to try new interests without committing to a whole new hobby, products they can customize, activities worth returning for, and convenient awareness of what is happening. Creators and brands want direct access to a relevant audience in an environment with a coherent identity. PinkOut aims to serve both sides within one destination.

## 3. Market, Audience, and Cultural Context

### 3.1 Geography

The launch market is **Riyadh, Saudi Arabia**. The exact property, address, opening date, confirmed schedule, and opening hours were **not established** in the current launch materials as of this knowledge compilation. Some concept copy mentions **Boulevard City** for a themed camping idea; that is not sufficient evidence that the PinkOut destination has a confirmed address there.

### 3.2 Primary audience

The experience is designed **primarily for women**, especially teens, university-age women, young adults, and groups of friends interested in beauty, fashion, self-expression, creativity, wellness, shopping, entertainment, and social discovery. The concept presentation describes a youthful but mature environment, with sculptural furniture, tactile materials, and fashion-led creative direction.

### 3.3 Other relevant participants

The wider PinkOut ecosystem may involve professional women, creators, artists, performers, fitness teachers, workshop hosts, brands, local designers, retail tenants, food vendors, and visitors with different spending interests. The presentation also illustrates supporting environments named a **Kids Area** and **Men's Lounge**. These are **concept support spaces**, not evidence of finalized child, accompanying-male, or general admission eligibility.

### 3.4 Guest-policy nuance

One visual presentation calls PinkOut a **women-only social destination**. Later `berif.md` explicitly adopts **women-first / created for women** until the formal guest policy is approved. Both statements are part of the project's history, but the latter is the safer current description of public positioning. Age restrictions, children, companions, and the role of any support area are unconfirmed operational matters.

### 3.5 Local language

Arabic is the primary language; English is the secondary language. PinkOut's name and English zone names are brand assets that can appear alongside natural Arabic descriptions. The Arabic experience should be culturally native and able to use feminine second-person forms, for example **اكتشفي، انضمي، اصنعي، عيشي**. This is a language identity, not a fixed website-only design mandate.

## 4. The Physical World and Spatial Concept

### 4.1 Scale and status

The smaller concept deck presents an **approximately 2,000 m² indoor venue** organized around open circulation and a central gathering space. This is a **conceptual spatial assumption**, not a verified measurement of a signed and constructed property. The final presentation includes alternative floor plans and detailed environmental renders. Dimensions, footprint, engineering services, and site fit must be validated against the real venue before operations.

### 4.2 Spatial organizing idea

PinkOut is conceived as an **open, explorative journey rather than a strict sequence**. Guests can move according to their interests, while the environment visually connects the activities. **Rose Hub** anchors the social center. **PinkOut Live** occupies a visible stage position without overwhelming the venue. **Move Studio** is more enclosed to support privacy and programmed classes. Entrance and exit artwork make arrival and departure memorable parts of the experience. The market, beauty and workshop environments are relatively open and invite discovery.

### 4.3 Materials and environments

Recurring design characteristics include warm ivory floors and surfaces, pink architectural details, softly glowing arches, florals, mirrors, brushed metallic/chrome touches, colored upholstery, sculptural seating, translucent accents, tactile surfaces, and curated brand graphics. The intent is premium and lively, not a conventional pink-painted expo booth hall.

### 4.4 Operational ideas in the final presentation

The presentation's environmental principles include neutral, color-faithful lighting at faces and makeup stations; a continuously legible visitor circulation loop; acoustic management between stage and studios; movable furnishings and quick-change activity space; interchangeable brand/sponsor surfaces; and attention to queues, privacy, amenities, cleaning, and comfort. These are conceptual operating considerations rather than proof of implemented fit-out.

### 4.5 Possible physical support areas

A **Kids Area** and **Men's Lounge** are depicted in the final presentation. The implied rationale is to support visitor groups without changing PinkOut's women-first core. No final admissions, childcare, staffing, security, waiting-area, or companion-access rules can be inferred from the imagery alone.

## 5. The Seven Established PinkOut Destinations

The concept repeatedly identifies **seven primary destinations**. They are one connected ecosystem, not seven unrelated companies. Their names and core roles are foundational PinkOut knowledge.

| Destination | Central purpose | Key visitor experience |
|---|---|---|
| **Pink Passage** | Immersive entrance and exit | Arrive through art, light, mirrors and recognizable photo moments |
| **Rose Hub** | Social heart and rotating activation zone | Gather, enjoy the café, explore a changing nearby activation |
| **Beauty Lab** | Beauty, technology and personalization | Discover, test, personalize and experience beauty concepts |
| **Make Studio** | Hands-on creativity | Create a customized item and take it home |
| **Pink Market** | Retail, treats and lifestyle shopping | Browse curated brands, gifts, fashion, sweets and seasonal pop-ups |
| **Move Studio** | Movement and wellness | Join ballet, yoga, dance, fitness and related classes |
| **PinkOut Live** | Stage and entertainment | Watch performances, talks, podcasts, launches and community content |

The following descriptions retain the concepts from the PDF sources. Activity menus and equipment examples are **illustrative**, not verified as available on a particular opening day.

### 5.1 Pink Passage — the gateway

Also called **The Pink Portal** in a section of the concept presentations. Pink Passage gives PinkOut a dramatic beginning and ending. The entrance transforms the transition from the outside city into a highly designed, expressive world. Possible elements include an art gateway, layered arches, mirrors, lighting, interactive artworks, seasonal décor, signature colors, sculptural forms, floral treatment, and portrait-friendly spaces. The exit echoes the aesthetic and provides a final farewell moment.

**Experiential role:** First impression, identity, anticipation, photography, wayfinding, arrival ritual, and a memorable exit. This is more than a functional door.

### 5.2 Rose Hub — the social heart

Rose Hub is the central gathering point. Some earlier materials call the central social area **Pink Heart** or **Central Hub**. The standardized primary label is **Rose Hub**.

Its core elements are a planned **PinkOut Café** and an adjoining **changeable activation area**. Café ideas include a matcha bar, matcha preparation machine, coffee, signature PinkOut beverages, and seasonal drinks. The adjacent space might become a beauty or fashion launch, product reveal, pop-up activity, e-sports experience, small sports/padel-inspired installation, or photo space. Its changing character is fundamental to PinkOut's repeat-visit appeal.

**Experiential role:** Meeting friends, resting, enjoying food and drinks, watching the central space change, and transitioning between different parts of the venue.

**Important distinction:** The conceptual PinkOut Café is part of the venue vision, but the final drinks menu, operating partner, pricing and opening status have not been verified.

### 5.3 Beauty Lab — discovery and personalization

Beauty Lab combines playful discovery with emerging beauty technology and branded product experiences. The concept emphasizes **participation and personalization**, not simply purchasing a ready-made product or booking a salon appointment.

Proposed experiences include:

- **Personalized Foundation Lab:** A system for shade/complexion analysis to help create a tailored foundation tone.
- **AI Fragrance Lab:** Visitor preferences and fragrance notes inform a personalized scent experience.
- **Custom Makeup & Nail Lab:** Technology-assisted selection or creation of tailored makeup and nail colors.
- **Brand Boutique:** Rotating displays, local and international beauty brands, exclusive collections, launches and creator collaborations.

Concept illustrations show visual merchandising, kiosks, product testing, hands-on counters and beauty content. Technology descriptions are **concept claims**; they are not evidence that a specific machine is installed, clinically validated, commercially available, or capable of any medical diagnosis.

**Experiential role:** Experimenting, learning, creating a personal result, discovering brands, and participating in beauty culture.

### 5.4 Make Studio — hands-on making

The Make Studio tagline is **“Create it. Personalize it. Take it home.”** It is a workshop environment that blends creative play, skilled instruction, and personalization equipment.

Proposed stations include:

1. **Bracelet & Charm Bar** — visitors assemble accessories from beads and charms.
2. **Phone Case Studio** — personalized phone-case designs and embellishments.
3. **Laser Engraving** — initials, names, and custom graphics added to selected items.
4. **Embroidery Studio** — names, patterns, and graphic customization on textiles.
5. **Custom Candy** — personalized sweets and cotton candy forms.

The visitor becomes a maker. The finished product functions as a meaningful keepsake and a shareable piece of content.

**Experiential role:** Agency, craft, creativity, memory-making, giftability, and taking a tangible result home.

### 5.5 Pink Market — discovery-driven shopping

Pink Market is imagined as a **small lifestyle neighborhood inside PinkOut**, with approachable storefronts, open displays, market counters, curated tenants, and seasonal pop-ups. Its character is dynamic and discoverable rather than a static set of exhibition booths.

Possible market categories include **sweets and desserts**, **ice cream and treats**, **fashion and accessories**, **gifts and lifestyle**, and **seasonal/limited-edition pop-ups**. Sample products include pastries, chocolate, candy, fashion by local designers, jewelry, bags, teddy bears, flowers, and distinctive gift items.

**Experiential role:** Browsing, discovery, gifting, snacking, local-brand exposure, and small surprises on each visit.

**Commercial role:** Retailer placement, brand exposure, tenant rent and possible seller commissions are discussed in the business models, but no specific tenant inventory is confirmed.

### 5.6 Move Studio — movement and wellness

A semi-private or enclosed environment for programmed activities including **ballet, yoga, dance, fitness**, and other wellness-oriented sessions. The architectural presentation illustrates mirrors, bars, mats, natural lighting, plant details, and a softer environment for movement and instruction.

**Experiential role:** Learning, self-expression through movement, wellness, classes, focused group participation, and community formation. The studio's more enclosed layout supports class privacy and sound separation from the main stage.

**Unresolved:** Class schedule, instructors, age eligibility, price, registration requirements and equipment are not confirmed.

### 5.7 PinkOut Live — entertainment and content

PinkOut Live is a **flexible stage and audience environment**. The stage accommodates intimate talks and bigger performances, while audience furnishing can vary: low tables, lounge chairs, bean bags, poufs and temporary standing zones may be reconfigured according to the program.

Its content categories include **live performances, DJ sessions, artist appearances, creator talks, podcasts, panel discussions, beauty/fashion/product launches, community programs, and special events**.

**Experiential role:** Shared social energy, entertainment, discovery of talent, branded moments, content creation, and reasons to return. The stage is not just an auditorium; it is a programmable cultural focal point.

## 6. How a PinkOut Visit Is Envisioned

A visitor journey example in `PinkOut_FinalPresentation.pdf` breaks the day into **arrive → explore → create → gather → return**. This describes the intended experience, not an operational check-in workflow that is already live.

1. **Arrive.** See the day's creative theme and pass through the art-driven gateway.
2. **Explore.** Wander through the market, beauty activities, installations, creator or photo experiences, and open zones according to personal interest.
3. **Create.** Make an object, try a new look, take a class, take part in a challenge, or discover a new product.
4. **Gather.** Meet friends around Rose Hub, the café, or the live stage; watch a talk, performance, or changing activation.
5. **Return.** Discover a new theme, instructor, workshop, visiting creator, featured brand, or event on a future occasion.

The central insight is **exploration without forced routing**. PinkOut is meant to support visitors who spend a short casual visit and others who participate in multiple ticketed and unticketed experiences. A first visit can create emotional attachment; new programming is meant to sustain it.

### 6.1 Moments that make PinkOut memorable

- A recognizable gateway and a sense of entering a different world.
- Photo/video-friendly environments without reducing the whole experience to photo booths.
- Making or customizing an item instead of only purchasing an item.
- Meeting people through scheduled classes, talks, activities, and shared interests.
- Discovering rotating lifestyle brands.
- Feeling connected with the PinkOut name through the same app and communication channels after leaving.

### 6.2 Participation and possible payment layers

The current launch brief distinguishes **general admission** from some **premium or separately paid activities**. The planned general entry figure is **SAR 35**. Which classes, items, activities, products or experiences are included versus individually paid depends on the eventual operational and commercial program. General admission should not be interpreted as an all-inclusive pass.

## 8. Community, Talent, Creators, and Partners

### 8.1 A platform for participants, not only an audience

PinkOut is intended to help female talent, creators, instructors, performers and businesses showcase work and build ongoing communities. This is one of the three formal project pillars. PinkOut therefore has two meaningful kinds of participation: **visitors who experience and engage**, and **partners/talent who create, host, operate, perform or sell**.

### 8.2 Creator and talent categories

Project materials mention, or naturally group, the following participation types: performers, DJs, singers/musicians, artists, speakers, podcast hosts, content creators, beauty professionals, fitness and dance instructors, workshop facilitators, designers, small brands, and retailers. Each is a potential programming or commercial partner—not necessarily an employed PinkOut team member.

### 8.3 Commercial partner categories

Beauty, fashion, food and beverage, wellness, technology, lifestyle retail, entertainment, product innovation, local design and gifting businesses all fit the concept. The venue may offer product launches, experiences, sponsorship placements, pop-up stores, workshops, brand takeovers and content collaborations.

### 8.4 Role of rotating brands

In concept renders, recognizable commercial brands may appear in displays as **styling and sponsorship examples**. The presentation explicitly says that **Sephora, SHEIN and coffee brands were discussed as examples and no sponsorship or partnership is assumed confirmed**. The same caution applies to any other logo or brand name appearing in concept art.

### 8.5 Long-term community logic

The intention is for PinkOut's relationship with visitors and creators to persist between visits. Event recaps, announcements, discoverable creators, future workshops, relevant activities, personal favorites, and membership or loyalty ideas could eventually contribute to that continuity. At present this is a **product aspiration**, not evidence of an existing active social network or functioning membership program.

## 9. The Digital PinkOut Ecosystem

PinkOut has a planned relationship between **a physical experience**, **a mobile app**, and **a public website**. These are parts of one brand, but they serve distinct purposes.

### 9.1 The conceptual relationship

**Awareness and first discovery → PinkOut app relationship → physical PinkOut visit → recurring engagement and future activations.**

This is the project's stated business journey, not a claim that all elements currently operate. The long-term digital proposition matters because PinkOut does not want its connection with a visitor to end at the venue exit.

### 9.2 The mobile app's role

The mobile app is described as PinkOut's **primary functional digital product**. The PDF mockup and the current documentation contemplate:

- Discovering the seven physical destinations and individual experiences.
- Browsing what is happening **today** and the coming schedule.
- Viewing classes, workshops, performances, live sessions and activities.
- Reserving eligible experiences and appointments.
- Selecting a date, time and possible guest count as part of a reservation flow.
- Viewing or managing eligible tickets and participation details.
- Saving favorite events, activities or destinations.
- Receiving news, confirmations, reminders, and programming updates.
- Staying connected to PinkOut after the initial physical season or event.
- Potential future memberships, exclusive releases, rewards, loyalty or beauty-related services.

The existence of app interface mockups in the presentation is not proof that a public app has been launched, tested or published. Store links and final product-release timing are unconfirmed.

### 9.3 App concepts discussed in project chats

A prior project conversation requested distinct iPhone screen images for **Home**, **Today**, and **PinkOut Live**. Their intended meanings within PinkOut are:

| Concept | Knowledge context | Evidence/status |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | A central personalized discovery point for destinations, featured events and service/experience access | Requested mockup concept; the final presentation also illustrates a homepage |
| **Today** | A time-sensitive view of the day's happenings, schedule and relevant activities | Requested mockup concept; not necessarily a confirmed primary navigation tab |
| **PinkOut Live** | A digital way to explore live-stage entertainment and changing content | Requested mockup concept; PinkOut Live is also one of the seven **physical** destinations |

The design system contains an initial app-navigation suggestion of **Home, Discover, Schedule, Saved, Profile**, explicitly presented as adaptable. Therefore, **Today** and **PinkOut Live** should be understood as valuable app/content concepts rather than automatically assumed to be the five final navigation labels.

### 9.4 What the public website represents

The website is the public-facing introduction to the PinkOut world. The current document `berif.md` describes a **one-page prelaunch website** intended for brand discovery, waitlist interest, app anticipation, and commercial/talent enquiries. It explains the world and the experiences, while the app is intended to hold actual transactional/personal participation workflows.

The website is conceptually a **brand gateway**, not the entire PinkOut operating system. Its scope may evolve once the venue and app become operational. The website brief's proposed sections, contact forms, and calls-to-action are **current communications planning**, not intrinsic attributes that define the PinkOut brand permanently.

### 9.5 Prelaunch versus active operation

**Prelaunch:** Riyadh coming-soon messaging, discovery of the concept and its destinations, interest/waitlist registration, partnership enquiries, and introduction of the app.

**When real programming is live:** Public channels can communicate verified opening details, published events, news and active app-store links, subject to actual product readiness.

**Longer term:** The brand may extend to recurring membership, loyalty, new seasons, offsite/pop-up experiences and additional services. These ideas are not assumed to have launched.

### 9.6 Privacy and trust within the digital relationship

If visitor contact details, reservations, schedules, payments, preferences or communications are processed, PinkOut will need a clear operating and privacy model. The project design system treats Arabic-first access, accessibility and honest states as product quality. External beauty-marketplace strategy documents analyze more advanced payment/provider privacy questions; those are **future research** and should not be confused with an existing PinkOut backend or approved policy.

## 10. Brand Personality, Storytelling, and Communication

### 10.1 Personality

The agreed character is **premium but approachable; playful but not childish; highly visual; mature, youthful, fashion-led, social, creative, expressive, and women-first**. A playful youth-facing world can still have disciplined typography, beautiful environmental design, clean navigation and intentional storytelling.

### 10.2 Narrative themes

The strongest recurring narrative is **“Your Own World.”** A visitor should feel free to explore multiple interests without choosing just one identity. PinkOut celebrates combinations: beauty and movement, making and shopping, laughter and performance, social time and individual expression.

Other recurring themes include:

- **Discover:** Find something you had not expected.
- **Create:** Make a thing, experiment with a look, share an idea.
- **Connect:** Meet friends, creators, and people with shared interests.
- **Move:** Dance, stretch, learn, express yourself physically.
- **Play:** Participate in themes and friendly challenges.
- **Return:** The place changes even though its identity remains familiar.

- “A place to create, connect and come back to.”

These are source-supported messages or source-proposed editorial lines. They are not proof of trademark registration or a legally protected slogan.

## 11. Identity, Logo and Visual World

### 11.1 Logo decision and history

`PinkOut_FinalPresentation.pdf` begins with **Logo Option 1** and **Logo Option 2**. Option 1 is a chunky typographic mark with a “Your Own World” tagline. **Logo Option 2** is a more expressive, script-like PinkOut wordmark with a heart-shaped element. The later `design-system.md` and `berif.md` select **Option 2** as the official current creative direction. The logo shown at the start of `pinkOUT.pdf` also features this expressive style.

The chosen logo is an asset/image design, **not** something that can be faithfully recreated by choosing a script font and typing “PinkOut”. Accurate usage depends on having the proper approved asset variant.

### 11.5 Architecture as identity

Arches and portal silhouettes recur across the presentation's exterior, entrance and interior renders. Other recurring motifs include glowing edges, ribbons or drapery, florals, portrait-framing shapes, sculptural seating, stylized hearts, campaign display panels, and polished physical materials. This creates a recognizable PinkOut world that functions as a place, a photographic backdrop, a digital brand, and a launch storytelling asset.

### 11.6 Three exterior facade concepts

The final presentation illustrates **three alternative creative directions**, not three simultaneously confirmed buildings:

1. **Floral Ribbon:** Deep-green frontage with pink sculptural drapery/ribbon, florals, illustrated figure, illuminated arch and beauty/fashion campaign panels.
2. **Crystal Gallery:** Blush/pink, theatrical facade with crystal features, tall luminous arches, warm metallic trim and dramatic displays.
3. **Garden Portal:** Dark-green base, layered flowers, soft pink lighting, branded campaign panels and flower-wrapped arch.

The later `pinkOUT.pdf` especially emphasizes a pink floral/deep-green portal presentation. These are **design renderings**, not photographic documentation of a completed exterior.

### 11.7 Photography and content art direction

The design system distinguishes two relevant visual registers:

**PinkOut World:** Cinematic, carefully staged or rendered environments, campaign imagery, immersive spaces, warm face lighting, architectural texture, and premium editorial composition. Used to communicate the ambition and physical identity.

**PinkOut Community:** Authentic, polished candid coverage of visitors, creators, learning, interaction, workshops, social gatherings, and activity recaps. This corresponds to lived experiences once there is real approved content.

Natural skin-tone accuracy matters especially in Beauty Lab imagery. Pink environmental lighting can be expressive, but product colors and complexions should remain believable. Concept renders and real documentary photography must not be misrepresented as one another.

### 11.8 Motion and digital atmosphere

The design system describes soft entrances, controlled reveals, restrained parallax, portal-inspired transitions, smooth image movement and subtle card interaction, while discouraging exhausting bouncing, spinning decorations or overwhelming effects. This is design-language knowledge; specific websites or apps can choose appropriate implementations.

### 11.9 Accessibility as an identity value

The shared design system targets **WCAG 2.2 AA** and treats accessibility as part of a premium experience. It specifically notes that normal white text on Signature Pink has inadequate contrast. It emphasizes appropriate contrast, focus visibility, screen-reader meaning, responsive text, reduction of unnecessary motion, comfortable touch targets and properly engineered Arabic RTL. The values are part of PinkOut's design direction independent of a particular build framework.

## 12. Business Model and Commercial Logic

### 12.1 Why commercial opportunity is part of the concept

PinkOut is envisioned as a destination with more than one economic engine. Entry revenue alone would not capture the value created by changing retail, experiences, sponsorships and community. The project presentations therefore discuss a **multi-stream commercial model** that can support both a launch season and longer-term activities.

Potential revenue sources named in the presentations include:

- General admission during ticketed operating periods.
- Annual and seasonal sponsorships.
- Pop-up store, market, retail-unit or booth leasing.
- Paid workshops, experiences, classes, and event participation.
- Courses and possible subscriptions after a season.
- Full-venue rental for private events.
- Birthday parties and small group celebrations.
- Schools and organized group visits.
- PinkOut-branded merchandise.
- Photo and printed-photo products.
- Studio rental by the hour.
- Sales commissions from retail merchants.
- Optional extras attached to private bookings.

These are **commercial concepts**, not a verified list of products already for sale, collected revenue, signed tenants, or sponsorship commitments.

### 12.2 General admission

The working launch brief identifies **SAR 35** as the **planned general entry price**. It describes selected activities as included and others as potentially priced separately. The final approved ticket catalog, cancellation rules, taxes or fees, complimentary entries, age brackets, and inclusions remain unconfirmed.

### 12.3 Sponsor and brand value

PinkOut may provide value through a relevant women-first audience, visual identity, content creation, direct product trials, social experiences, launch stages, rotating retail settings, branded activations and creator partnerships. The proposition is experiential: a sponsor or tenant can **join the PinkOut world**, not merely purchase a rectangle of advertising space.

## 16. Frequently Asked PinkOut Knowledge Questions

**What is PinkOut?**  
PinkOut is a women-first lifestyle, creativity, entertainment and community destination conceived for Riyadh, with a continuing digital world centered on its app.

**Is PinkOut only about beauty?**  
No. Beauty Lab is one of seven destinations. Movement, workshops, shopping, live stage content, the café/social hub and immersive artwork are all major parts of the concept.

**How many destinations are there?**  
Seven core destinations: Pink Passage, Rose Hub, Beauty Lab, Make Studio, Pink Market, Move Studio and PinkOut Live.

**Why would somebody return?**  
Because the physical environments retain a recognizable identity while the themes, creators, pop-ups, sessions, workshops and stage programs change.

**Is PinkOut an event or a permanent platform?**  
Its **vision** is a continuing destination and community platform, not a single one-off event. Specific seasons, operating periods, premises and app functionality still require confirmation.

**Does PinkOut have an app?**  
The project includes an app strategy and design mockups. The sources do not establish a live downloadable release or verified store links.

**What is the website for?**  
The current prelaunch communication plan uses it to explain PinkOut, build interest, gather a waitlist, and introduce partnership opportunities and the app. The website is not itself the entire product.

**What is the planned entry price?**  
The working launch brief says **SAR 35 general entry**, with some potential additional paid experiences. Final on-sale conditions and inclusions are not established.

**Where exactly is PinkOut opening?**  
The agreed city is **Riyadh**. A final address is not established in the sources. Mentions of Boulevard City in a themed programming example are not proof of venue location.

**When does it open?**  
The working brief says “coming soon” and gives approximate planning timing, but not an approved public opening date.

**Is PinkOut women-only? Can children or men enter?**  
The concept is strongly women-focused. One earlier presentation says women-only, but the latest website brief deliberately uses women-first pending final admissions policy. Children/companions and conceptual support spaces cannot be treated as confirmed eligibility rules.

**Are Sephora or SHEIN confirmed partners?**  
No evidence of confirmation appears in the project sources. Both are used as example possible collaborations in the concept presentation.

**Does PinkOut offer home beauty booking?**  
Not as an established current offering. There is related market research and a later-phase possibility, but it is separate from the core launch concept.

**What is the visual identity?**  
Selected Logo Option 2, Signature Pink `#F17DBD`, Logo Blush `#FEBEBC`, Deep Green `#03312E`, Warm Ivory `#F1EBE2`, Brushed Metal `#B8BBBD`, arches, expressive feminine editorial settings, and Alexandria as the core interface typeface.

**Is PinkOut already profitable, or do the decks show guaranteed profit?**  
Neither. The deck provides internal scenarios for costs, revenue and estimated profit. They are not historical or audited results.
